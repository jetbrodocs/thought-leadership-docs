## 3. Rohan's Personal POV — What He Actually Believes

These are the positions Rohan holds and will stake his personal reputation on. Every post should be rooted in at least one of these.

**Belief 1: Technology is useless if it does not positively impact the business.**
Net effect is the only metric that matters. If a system was delivered, launched, and celebrated — but the business is not measurably better — the project failed. Full stop. The industry is full of technology that was technically impressive and operationally irrelevant. Rohan calls this out directly.

**Belief 2: Tech should work silently.**
The best systems are the ones nobody talks about — because there is nothing to complain about. Technology that powers operations without friction, without crashes, without workarounds, without daily firefighting. That is the standard. Vanity tech — flashy interfaces, overengineered architectures, impressive demos that fall apart in production — is a distraction and often a risk.

**Belief 3: Technology does not have to be expensive or out of reach.**
The belief that custom, high-quality technology is only accessible to enterprises with large consulting budgets is a myth perpetuated by large firms whose business model depends on it. Rohan has seen what is possible at a fraction of the cost when engineering is paired with a realistic understanding of what the business actually needs.

**Belief 4: Process over results, every day.**
Results are a lagging indicator. They follow process, discipline, and the right habits executed consistently over time. Chasing results directly — in sales, in technology delivery, in building a team — leads to shortcuts, hype, and eventual failure. This applies to how Jetbro works, how Rohan sells, and how high-performing teams in any domain operate.

**Belief 5: Realistic solutions beat brilliant ones.**
The goal is not to deliver the most technically sophisticated solution. The goal is to deliver the solution that is aligned with the business reality, can be maintained by the team that inherits it, and will still be working reliably in five years. No unnecessary complexity. No hype cycles. Systems designed to make operations smoother, decisions clearer, and growth more controlled.

**Belief 6: Full-stack engineering paired with product-minded business thinking is rare and undervalued.**
Most technology firms have one or the other. Engineers who cannot translate technical decisions into business outcomes. Business consultants who cannot evaluate whether a proposed solution is realistic. The combination — someone who understands both the code and the commercial reality it serves — is where the real value sits. This is what Rohan brings and what Jetbro is built on.

## 4. The Credibility Foundation — Rohan's Story

Rohan's background does not follow a straight line — and that is exactly what makes it credible.

He studied supply chain and logistics, which gave him a ground-level understanding of how operations actually work in Indian enterprises — the complexity, the workarounds, the gap between how processes are supposed to run and how they actually do. This is not academic knowledge. It is the foundation of his ability to look at a business operation and immediately identify where technology can help and where it will make things worse.

From there, he moved into product management — learning how to translate business requirements into something buildable, how to prioritise ruthlessly, and how to think about what users actually need versus what they say they need. The product-minded thinking that Jetbro is known for starts here.

Then he founded a startup. The idea was genuinely novel. It failed. He will tell you that the failure taught him more than anything else in his career — about what it actually takes to sell something nobody has heard of, what happens when distribution is an afterthought, and what founders feel when they are on the other side of a vendor pitch trying to solve a problem with limited capital and even more limited time. This is the empathy that makes his voice different. He is not advising from a position of unbroken success. He has been through the hard version.

He eventually found his way into digital transformation and technology at Jetbro — where his supply chain background, product thinking, and founding experience converged into something useful. And simultaneously, he held a consulting post in the Sales function at Safal, an enterprise SaaS company — which put him on the client side of a technology engagement. He has sat in the chair that his own clients sit in. He knows what it feels like to evaluate a vendor, to be oversold, to receive a polished presentation followed by a difficult delivery.

This is the full picture: supply chain practitioner, product manager, failed founder, technology operator, and client-side consultant. The combination is rare. It is the reason Rohan can speak credibly to founders, operators, engineers, and buyers — because at some point, he has been all of them.

## 7. Content Formats

Different formats serve different purposes. The mix below is calibrated to what is sustainable for Rohan's role and what feels natural to his voice.

| Format | Purpose | Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Text post (short)** | Quick opinion, observation, or reaction. 100–300 words. | 3x per week | Primary format. Fast to produce, high engagement when sharp. |
| **Text post (long-form story)** | A narrative with a beginning, middle, and lesson. 400–800 words. | 1x per week | These tend to get shared. Use for career stories, client lessons, F1/aviation observations. |
| **Long-form article** | Deep dive on a specific topic. 800–1500 words. Published on LinkedIn or website. | 2x per month | For Pillars 1, 3, and 6 where the argument needs room to breathe. |
| **Carousel** | Visual framework, list, or step-by-step breakdown. | 2x per month | Useful for GTM frameworks, process breakdowns, and "X things I've learned" formats. |
| **Loom / screenshare** | Async video walkthrough — product demo, behind-the-scenes, or commentary on something on screen. | As relevant | Low production bar. Record, share, done. Good for Phlo/Plicony GTM content as the products develop. |
| **Repost with commentary** | React to something in the industry, a race result, an article, or a peer's post with a Rohan-specific take. | As relevant | Quick to produce and shows range. Always add original commentary — never repost without one. |

**What Rohan avoids:**
- Polished, high-production video — too high effort for the current stage
- Content that sounds like it was written by a marketing team
- Inspirational quotes or generic "lessons" that are not tied to a real experience

**The format test:** If the format requires more than 30 minutes to produce on a regular week, it should not be in the default rotation. Reserve longer-effort formats (long-form articles, carousels) for moments when the idea is genuinely worth it.

## 8. Ground Rules

Every post Rohan publishes under his personal name must meet these standards.

**Rule 1: It must be true.**
Not polished. Not optimised. True. If Rohan did not actually believe it or experience it, it does not go out under his name. The credibility of a personal brand is built on authenticity and destroyed by performance.

**Rule 2: Take a position.**
Every post must have a clear point of view. "Both sides have merit" is not a post — it is a non-answer. Rohan takes a position and defends it. He can be wrong. Being wrong publicly and acknowledging it is more credible than never committing to anything.

**Rule 3: One idea per post.**
A post that tries to cover three points covers none. Each post makes one argument, tells one story, or asks one question.

**Rule 4: Lead with the most interesting line.**
The first sentence determines whether anyone reads the second. It should be the most surprising, specific, or provocative thing in the post — not a preamble.

**Rule 5: Earned vulnerability only.**
Sharing failures and struggles is powerful — but only when it comes with a genuine lesson, not just for relatability. "I failed and here's what I learned" is valuable. "I failed and it was hard" is not.

**Rule 6: No corporate language.**
If a sentence could appear in a press release, it should be rewritten. Rohan writes the way he speaks to a peer he respects.

**Rule 7: No series until something earns it.**
No recurring named series until a topic or format consistently gets strong engagement. Let the audience tell Rohan what they want more of — then build a series around it.

## 9. Adaptation Framework

Rohan's content strategy is not fixed. It should evolve based on what is actually working.

**Weekly habit (5 minutes):**
After every post, note whether it overperformed or underperformed relative to expectation. Do not obsess — just notice. Keep a running list of "posts that hit" and "posts that missed" and what was different.

**Monthly review (20 minutes):**
- Which pillar got the most engagement this month?
- Which format performed best?
- Did any post generate a DM, a conversation, or a lead? Document it.
- One thing to do more of next month.
- One thing to do less of.

**Quarterly review (45 minutes):**
- Has the follower count grown meaningfully?
- Is the content mix still aligned with the 6 pillars, or has it drifted?
- Has anything earned the right to become a series?
- What is the most common reaction Rohan gets — and does it match the positioning he is building toward?
- Update this strategy document with any changes.

**The signal that matters most:**
A peer, prospect, or client mentions a specific post in a real conversation. That is the signal that the content is landing with the right people. Track every occurrence.

## 13. Cadence and Discipline

Rohan's personal publishing cadence is separate from Jetbro's. Given his role as COO and Head of Sales, it needs to be realistic and sustainable.

| Content Type | Frequency |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn posts (any format) | 4x per week |
| Long-form article or story | 2x per month |
| F1 / aviation / passion topic post | 1x per week (counts within 4x) |
| Content idea captured in backlog | Ongoing — capture immediately when the thought occurs |

**Non-negotiables:**

- Never go more than 4 days without a post
- Capture ideas in real time — the best post ideas come in the middle of client calls, while watching a race, or reading something that triggers a reaction. Write them down immediately.
- Every post ends with something that invites a response — a question, a position, an invitation to disagree
