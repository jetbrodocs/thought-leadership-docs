# Jetbro Thought Leadership Strategy
**Version:** 1.0 | **Owner:** Rohan Patel | **Last Updated:** April 2026

## 1. Purpose

Jetbro has built real credibility over 9 years through client work alone. Thought leadership is how we make that credibility visible to people who have not yet worked with us.

The goal is not to go viral. The goal is to be the most credible voice in the room when a CTO or Founder at a mid-to-large Indian enterprise is searching for a technology partner. When that person Googles Jetbro, looks up Rohan or Sharva on LinkedIn, or hears our name in a referral conversation — they should find something substantive that confirms we are the right choice.

Thought leadership also shortens the sales cycle. Prospects who have read something we published before the first call are already partially sold on our POV. They arrive curious, not skeptical.

## 2. The Jetbro POV — What We Actually Believe

These are not marketing messages. These are genuine positions Jetbro holds that most of the market would push back on. Every content piece should be rooted in at least one of these.

**Belief 1: Off-the-shelf fails Indian enterprises — and nobody says it clearly.**
Indian business processes, especially in procurement, operations, and supply chain, are too complex and too context-specific for packaged software to cover. Zoho, Salesforce, SAP — they work for the use cases they were designed for. But Indian mid-to-large enterprises routinely work around them, build parallel Excel systems, and create shadow processes. The result is digital adoption without digital transformation. We believe some degree of customisation is not a luxury — it is a prerequisite for genuine transformation in the Indian context.

**Belief 2: Custom development does not have to cost what Infosys charges.**
The conventional belief is that custom = expensive = only for the largest enterprises. We reject this. **The exorbitant pricing of large IT firms is a function of their overhead, not the work itself**. There is a viable middle ground: custom-built systems delivered at a price point that mid-market Indian enterprises can justify. That is exactly where Jetbro operates, and where Phlo and Plicony are being built.

**Belief 3: Digital transformation in India is a misunderstood word.**
Executives hear "digital transformation" and picture a 3-year, multi-crore engagement with a Big 4 firm that restructures their entire technology stack. That framing — pushed by large consultants for obvious commercial reasons — makes transformation feel inaccessible. We believe the right approach is smaller, faster, targeted interventions that deliver measurable impact within weeks or months. Build trust first. Go deeper once trust is earned.

**Belief 4: Technology firms should own KPIs, not just manage teams.**
Most technology partners manage delivery. They ensure the sprint is running, the code is shipped, the pod is staffed. Very few take ownership of whether the system they built actually moved the business metric it was supposed to move. We believe this accountability gap is the primary reason transformation projects fail. Jetbro is more ingrained in the product and system roadmap than a typical delivery partner — we provide the technology thought leadership that guides systems from their current state to a future state where they are genuinely more useful for the business.

**Belief 5: AI should make enterprises more productive today, not just prepare them for tomorrow.**
Most AI conversations in the enterprise are about strategy, readiness, and future potential. We are more interested in what AI can do right now — specifically in giving each executive and team member the ability to build productivity agents that reduce friction in their daily work. Plicony is built on this belief. The opportunity is not in replacing humans. It is in removing the repetitive, low-value work that prevents talented people from doing their best work.

## 3. Target Audience

Jetbro's thought leadership speaks to two audiences simultaneously.

**Primary: The Business Buyer**
CTO, Founder, COO, Head of IT at mid-to-large Indian enterprises (200–2000 employees) in supply chain, consumer apps, enterprise SaaS, food-tech, FMCG, and healthcare. These are the people who ultimately approve technology investment. They care about outcomes, business risk, and whether a partner can be trusted.

**Secondary: The Technical Validator**
Senior Engineers, Architects, and Heads of Engineering who evaluate the technical credibility of a potential partner. They care about how we build, what we believe about system design, and whether our technical opinions are sound.

**Geographic focus:** India (Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, Pune, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad). Australian and Canadian enterprise market is secondary for now.

**What this means for content:** Every piece should either speak to the business impact angle (for the primary buyer) or the technical credibility angle (for the validator). Very few pieces need to do both — that is what Rohan's personal profile is for. Jetbro, the company brand should lean toward business impact, outcomes, and POV.

## 4. Content Pillars

Jetbro will own three core content pillars and one supporting pillar. Every piece of content maps to one of these.

---

### Pillar 1: The Indian Enterprise Technology Reality
*What we believe about how technology should work in the Indian enterprise context*

This is Jetbro's sharpest differentiator in content form. Most technology content is written for the US or European market and transplanted to India. Jetbro speaks specifically to the Indian enterprise reality — the complexity of Indian business processes, the limitations of off-the-shelf software in this context, and what actually works.

**Topics within this pillar:**

**Topics within this pillar:**

*How enterprises can use AI to get things done today, not in 3 years*

This pillar positions Jetbro ahead of the curve without being speculative or hype-driven. The tone is always: practical, specific, already happening. This is where Plicony's narrative lives.

**Topics within this pillar:**

## 5. Tone of Voice

Jetbro's content voice is defined by these qualities — in order of priority:

- **Authoritative** — We speak from experience, not theory. We have done this work. We have seen what fails. We state positions confidently without hedging.
- **Direct** — No padding, no preamble. Get to the point. Respect the reader's time.
- **Revealing** — We share what others don't say. The uncomfortable truths about why transformation fails, what off-the-shelf really costs, what large consultants won't tell you.
- **Accessible** — Complex ideas explained simply. No jargon without plain-English translation. A CTO and a business owner should both be able to follow.
- **Approachable** — We are not broadcasting from a pedestal. We are sharing from experience. We welcome disagreement, questions, and conversation.
- **Proactive** — We don't wait to be asked. We publish what the market needs to hear before they know they need to hear it.

**What Jetbro never sounds like:** Corporate, vague, self-promotional, or like a press release.

## 6. Reference Voices — What We Learn From Each

**Thoughtworks**
What they do well: They publish genuine intellectual property — the Tech Radar, long-form technical opinions, and recurring series that practitioners actually use. They are cited, not just read. The lesson for Jetbro: create recurring, named content series that become expected and anticipated. A regular series builds a returning audience faster than standalone posts.

**Slalom**
What they do well: More accessible than McKinsey, highly case-study-driven. They make transformation feel achievable rather than overwhelming. The lesson for Jetbro: keep language accessible. Our audience is operators, not academics. Avoid jargon that sounds impressive but communicates nothing.

**McKinsey**
What they do well: Named research, proprietary data, and a consistent publishing cadence. Even a 2-page article from McKinsey feels authoritative because it is backed by original research or methodology. The lesson for Jetbro: over time, invest in original research — even small-scale surveys of Indian CTOs or an annual "State of Digital Transformation in India" report would build significant authority.

**Rahul Mathur (LinkedIn)**
What he does well: Consistent personal publishing that builds a recognisable voice over time. The lesson for Jetbro: consistency matters more than perfection. One good post per week, every week, compounds faster than three brilliant posts followed by three months of silence.

## 6. Content Formats

Different formats serve different purposes. Jetbro should use all of them, but not equally.

| Format                                     | Platform              | Purpose                                                             | Frequency    |
| ------------------------------------------ | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **Text Post**                              | LinkedIn              | Share a POV, provoke a reaction, start a conversation               | 3x per week  |
| **Carousel**                               | LinkedIn, Instagram   | Teach a framework, summarise a concept, make something visual       | 1x per week  |
| **Case Study (short-form)**                | LinkedIn, website     | Prove outcomes. 300-500 words. Outcome-led headline.                | 4x per month |
| **Long-form Article**                      | LinkedIn, website     | Build deep credibility on a specific topic. 800-1500 words.         | 1x per month |
| **Data / Observation Post**                | LinkedIn, X           | Share a stat, an observation, or a trend with Jetbro's commentary   | As relevant  |
| **Behind-the-scenes**                      | LinkedIn, Instagram   | Show how Jetbro works. Team, process, moments. Humanises the brand. | 1x per month |
| **Reposts with Commentary**                | LinkedIn, X           | React to industry news or a competitor's article with a Jetbro POV  | As relevant  |
| **Thread**                                 | X                     | Expand a POV into 5–8 connected points. High shareability in the Indian tech ecosystem. | 1x per week |
| **Short opinion post**                     | X                     | Strip a LinkedIn POV to its sharpest 1–3 lines. Fast to produce, reactive. | 3–4x per week |
| **Reel (60 seconds)**                      | Instagram             | Rohan or team sharing a quick opinion or behind-the-scenes moment. Low production, high authenticity. | 1x per week |

**What to avoid:**
- Generic inspirational content with no Jetbro-specific angle
- Technology announcements without an opinion attached
- Posts that are clearly promotional with no educational value
- Overproduced content that looks like advertising
- Copying the same post verbatim across platforms — each platform needs the idea adapted to its format and audience

## 6a. Platform Strategy — X (Twitter) and Instagram

### X (Twitter)

**Audience:** Indian startup founders, CTOs, engineers, VCs, and product people. Strong overlap with Jetbro's ICP — particularly ICP 2 (Executor) and ICP 3 (Builder). More community-oriented than LinkedIn; credibility here builds peer respect that feeds referrals over time.

**What works:**
- Short, punchy opinions — 1–3 sentences, strong POV, no hedging
- Threads — expand a LinkedIn post into 5–8 tweets. Works well for frameworks, debunks, and case study summaries
- Reactive commentary — when something happens in Indian tech, Jetbro's take on it posted within hours
- Engaging with other voices — replying to founders, CTOs, and industry figures Rohan respects. Visibility compounds faster through engagement than through posting alone

**What does not work:** Long corporate text, case study walls of text, anything that sounds like it was written for a brochure

**Content source:** Almost entirely repurposed from LinkedIn — strip a LinkedIn post to its sharpest line, that is a tweet. A LinkedIn long-form becomes a thread. Minimal net-new effort required.

**Cadence:** 1 thread/week + 3–4 short reactive posts

**Goal:** Credibility within the Indian startup and tech ecosystem. Peer respect that strengthens warm referrals. Not a direct lead gen channel in the short term.

**Audience reality check:** Jetbro's primary ICP (CTOs, Heads of IT at mid-market enterprises) is largely not on Instagram professionally. Instagram will not move the sales pipeline directly in the near term.

**Where Instagram does work for Jetbro:**
- **ICP 3 — The Builder:** Series B+ founders and consumer brand operators (D2C, food-tech, health-tech) are active here. This is a legitimate audience for Jetbro.

**Content source:** LinkedIn carousels reformatted for Instagram (minor sizing adjustment). Reels are net-new but low effort — no scripting, no editing beyond trimming.

**Cadence:** 3x per week — 2 carousels, 1 reel

**Timing:** Layer Instagram in 60 days after LinkedIn and X are running consistently. The visual production overhead is manageable once the content rhythm is established.

**Goal:** Brand warmth, ICP 3 reach, Rohan's personal audience growth. Not a pipeline channel — a credibility and discovery channel.

## 7. Ground Rules

Every piece of Jetbro content — regardless of format — must adhere to these standards.

**Rule 1: Every piece must have a point of view.**
We do not publish neutral summaries of industry trends. We take a position. If the content could have been written by any technology firm, it should not be published under the Jetbro name.

**Rule 2: Specific beats general, always.**
"We helped a food-tech company reduce delivery costs" is weak. "We rebuilt the dispatch logic for a Bengaluru food-tech company and reduced last-mile delivery costs by 20% in 8 weeks" is strong. Use numbers, timelines, and industry names wherever possible.

**Rule 3: Lead with the business, not the technology.**
Jetbro is not a technology company. It is a business outcomes company that uses technology. Every piece should be framed around what the business needed, not what we built.

**Rule 4: No jargon without explanation.**
If we use a technical term, we explain it in plain English in the same sentence or the next. Our business buyer audience is not always technical.

**Rule 5: One idea per post.**
LinkedIn posts that try to cover three ideas cover zero. Each post makes one clear argument.

**Rule 6: End with something worth responding to.**
Every post should either ask a question, state a position that invites disagreement, or share something that makes the reader want to share it with a colleague. Passive content gets passive results.

**Rule 7: Never post without a quality review.**
No piece goes live without being reviewed by Rohan or an approved reviewer. This applies even to short posts. One poorly worded post in front of the wrong audience does more damage than two weeks of silence.

## 8. Cadence and Discipline

Consistency is the entire game in early-stage thought leadership. The algorithm rewards regularity. The audience builds through repetition. The first 90 days will feel like publishing into silence — that is normal and expected.

**Target cadence for the Jetbro company page:**

| Content Type | Frequency |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn posts (any format) | 4x per week |
| Case study published | 4x per month |
| Long-form article | 1x per month |
| New content idea backlog review | Weekly (Monday) |
| Performance review | Monthly |
| Strategy review | Quarterly |

**Production workflow:**

```
Week N-1: Topic selected and brief written (AI agent or content person)
Day 1-2: First draft produced
Day 3: Rohan reviews and edits
Day 4: Final version approved
Day 5: Scheduled for publication
```

**Non-negotiables:**
- Never go more than 5 days without a post on the Jetbro page
- Every post must be scheduled at least 24 hours in advance — no last-minute rushed content
- If the pipeline runs dry, use the backlog (see Pillar topics above — there are 25+ topics already defined)

## 9. Adaptation Framework

Thought leadership strategy is not set-and-forget. We commit to regular review cycles.

**Monthly Review (Rohan, 30 minutes):**
- Which posts got the most engagement? Why?
- Which posts got the least? What was different?
- Did any post generate an inbound conversation or lead? Document it.
- Is the content mix balanced across pillars?
- One format or topic to double down on next month.
- One format or topic to drop or reduce next month.

**Quarterly Review (Rohan + content person, 60 minutes):**
- Is the overall POV landing? Are we getting comments that reflect people understanding our position?
- Has the follower count grown meaningfully?
- Have we received any speaking invitations, press mentions, or warm inbound?
- Do we need to add, remove, or adjust any content pillar?
- What are competitors or reference voices doing that we should be paying attention to?

**What we are watching for:**
- Posts that get saved or shared (more valuable than likes)
- Comments from the ICP audience (CTOs, Founders, Heads of IT)
- Direct messages referencing a post
- A prospect mentioning content in a discovery call — this is the gold standard signal

## 12. Case Study Process

**Volume:** 4 per month.

**Source:** Drawn from existing and past client work. Jetbro has 60+ client relationships to draw from. Start with the 5 case studies already on the website and expand from there.

**Client approval:** Not required as a standard step. Default approach is to anonymise client names and remove commercially sensitive details. If a client is willing to be named publicly, that is a bonus — ask selectively for clients who have the strongest outcomes and are most likely to agree.

**What to anonymise by default:**
- Client name (use industry + city descriptor instead, e.g. "a Mumbai-based supply chain company")
- Specific financial figures if they were shared in confidence
- Internal process details that could identify the client

**What to always include:**
- Industry and approximate company size
- The problem in plain English
- What Jetbro did (at a high level)
- The measurable outcome — with a number wherever possible

**Format:** 300–500 words. Outcome-led headline (the result, not the project). Written for the business buyer, not the technical validator.
