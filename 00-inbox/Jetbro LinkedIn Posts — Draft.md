## Post 1 — POV Opener
**Pillar:** Indian Enterprise Tech Reality
**Format:** Text post
**Status:** `Draft`

---

Most Indian enterprises haven't done digital transformation.

They've done digital decoration.

New software on top of old processes. A CRM nobody uses. An ERP that runs alongside 4 Excel sheets that do the actual work. A dashboard that pulls yesterday's data.

The technology is there. The transformation isn't.

Here's why: off-the-shelf software is built for Western business processes. Clean data, simple workflows, predictable operations. Indian enterprises — especially in supply chain, procurement, and operations — are more complex than that. The exceptions are the rule. The workarounds become the system.

So companies end up with two parallel realities: the software they bought, and the shadow processes their teams actually rely on.

That gap is expensive. It's slow. And it grows every year you don't address it.

The fix isn't always custom software. But it's rarely just buying another tool.

What does your team actually run on — the software, or the workarounds?

## Post 3 — Debunk
**Pillar:** Indian Enterprise Tech Reality
**Format:** Text post
**Status:** `Draft`

---

"Digital transformation" has become code for: expensive, slow, and painful.

Large consulting firms have spent 20 years making it feel that way. 3-year roadmaps. Multi-crore proposals. Transformation offices. Steering committees.

Most of it is overhead, not outcome.

Here's what we've seen actually work, across 9 years and 60+ client engagements:

Start with one broken thing. Fix it properly. Measure the result. Earn the right to go deeper.

A 6-week sprint that fixes a real operational problem builds more trust — and delivers more value — than a 6-month strategy engagement that produces a slide deck.

Transformation isn't a project with a start and end date. It's what happens when a business keeps making its technology incrementally more useful, quarter after quarter.

The companies that get this right don't announce transformation programmes. They just quietly get better.

Which do you trust more — a big plan or a small proof?

## Post 4 — AI Practical
**Pillar:** AI That Works Now
**Format:** Text post
**Status:** `Draft`

---

Most enterprise AI conversations are about readiness.

Are we ready for AI? Is our data clean enough? Do we have the right infrastructure?

Meanwhile, a head of operations at one of our clients is spending 3 hours every Monday manually compiling a report that 6 different systems could produce automatically.

That's not an AI readiness problem. That's a solvable problem — right now, with what already exists.

The highest-value AI use cases in Indian enterprises today aren't the big, flashy ones. They're the invisible ones: the report that gets compiled manually, the approval that sits in someone's inbox for 3 days, the data that gets copied from one system into another by a person who should be doing something more useful.

Productivity agents — small, task-specific automations — are where the real ROI is. Not because they're revolutionary, but because they remove friction that costs hours every week, at every level of the organisation.

AI strategy is fine. But what's the one manual process your team does every week that shouldn't require a human?

## Post 5 — Behind the Scenes
**Pillar:** How Jetbro Works
**Format:** Text post
**Status:** `Draft`

---

We turn down projects we haven't audited first.

Not because we're being difficult. Because the problem a client describes in the first conversation is almost never the whole problem.

They know something is broken. They can feel it in the slowdowns, the workarounds, the reports that take three days to compile. What they usually can't see is why — because the root cause is buried two or three layers below the symptom.

The paid audit is how we find it. 4 to 8 weeks, tightly scoped. We map what exists, trace where it breaks, and produce an architecture recommendation the client owns — regardless of what they decide to do next.

What it consistently reveals: the fix that seemed obvious at the start is rarely the right one. And the fix that actually works is almost always cheaper and faster than the client feared.

Skipping the diagnostic doesn't save time. It just moves the cost further down the project — where it's harder to fix and more expensive to undo.

The audit isn't a gate we put in the way. It's the thing that makes everything after it worth doing.
