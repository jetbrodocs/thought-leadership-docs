## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Jetbro's marketing function operates — what it does, who does it, on what cadence, and how it connects to sales. It is the operational counterpart to the Jetbro Thought Leadership Strategy, which defines *what* Jetbro says. This document defines *how* the machine runs.

Jetbro has grown entirely through referrals since 2017 with zero marketing investment. That is a strength — it proves the work speaks for itself. But referral-only growth has a ceiling, and Jetbro is at that ceiling. The 100% ARR growth target (INR 2 crores → INR 4 crores, FY 2026–27) cannot be achieved through referrals alone. Marketing is how Jetbro extends its reach beyond the existing network while reinforcing credibility for every warm introduction that comes through.

The goal is not awareness for its own sake. Marketing at Jetbro serves three purposes:
1. **Credibility** — when a prospect hears Jetbro's name, they find something substantive when they look us up
2. **Pipeline** — content and lead capture mechanisms generate qualified inbound, reducing dependence on referrals
3. **Retention** — existing clients who see Jetbro publishing consistently are reminded of the depth of what we know

## 2. Marketing Philosophy

**Content before campaigns.**
At a ₹25,000/month budget, paid advertising is not viable. Every rupee of marketing investment goes toward creating content that compounds over time — not ads that stop working the moment the spend stops.

**Organic reach, owned audiences.**
The goal is to build audiences that Jetbro owns: a newsletter subscriber list, a Substack following, a LinkedIn following. Platform algorithms change. Owned channels do not.

**Quality over volume.**
One well-crafted case study with a real outcome number is worth more than ten generic LinkedIn posts. We publish less and mean it more.

**Marketing feeds sales — not the other way around.**
Every piece of content should either build credibility with a prospect before a call, or generate a lead that enters the sales pipeline. Content for content's sake is waste.

**Lean and AI-assisted.**
The marketing function operates on a lean team: one internal team member running day-to-day execution, supported by the Jetbro AI agent (to be built) for drafting, repurposing, and scheduling. Rohan and Sharva provide strategic oversight and content review.

## 5. Content Workflows

All content at Jetbro moves through three sequential workflows. A piece of content does not enter production until it has cleared ingestion and screening. It does not publish until it has cleared content approval.

```
Workflow 1: Ingestion & Screening  →  Workflow 2: Content Production  →  Workflow 3: Content Approval
```

---

### Workflow 1: Ingestion & Screening

This workflow governs how raw inputs become defined, classified content briefs that are ready to enter production.

**Raw input can be anything:**
- A URL (article, LinkedIn post, news item, competitor piece)
- Text (a quote, a stat, a transcript, notes from a meeting)
- Audio (a recording, a voice note, a podcast episode)
- Video (a YouTube link, a Loom, a recorded client call)
- Any other raw format — the format does not matter at intake

**Stage 1 — Ingestion**
Raw content is submitted to Mahima in any format. There is no required format for submission — the goal is low friction at the point of capture so that nothing useful is lost. Team members, Rohan, and Sharva can all submit raw inputs. Mahima is the single inbox for all incoming material.

**Stage 2 — The What (Mahima decides)**
Mahima reviews the raw input and determines what content idea, angle, or post comes out of it. This is a creative and editorial decision. The raw input may become one post, several posts, a case study angle, or nothing at all if it does not serve any purpose. Mahima documents her decision in the Content Calendar with a one-line description of the content idea.

**Stage 3 — Classification**
Once "the what" is decided, Mahima classifies the content idea across two dimensions:

| Dimension | Options | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pillar** | Pillar 1 / Pillar 2 / Pillar 3 / Pillar 4 / Pillar 5 / Filler | Which content pillar does this idea most relate to? Filler if it maps to none. |
| **Topical Urgency** | High / Medium / Low | High = time-sensitive, publish within 48–72 hours. Medium = relevant for 1–2 weeks. Low = evergreen, no time pressure. |

**Stage 4 — Screening complete**
Once classified, the content idea is marked as "Screened" in the Content Calendar and is ready to enter Workflow 2 (Content Production). High urgency items go to the front of the production queue. Low urgency items join the standard backlog.

Nothing proceeds to production without completing the classification step. Unclassified content does not get produced.

## 6. Content Types

### 5.1 Case Studies
The most powerful sales tool Jetbro has. Real outcomes, real numbers, real work.

- **Format:** 300–500 words. Outcome-led headline. Problem → what Jetbro did → measurable result.
- **Volume:** 4 per month
- **Source:** Existing and past client work (60+ client relationships to draw from). See the **Case Study Building Pipeline** (separate document) for the full production process.
- **Where they live:** Primary: jetbro.in/expertise. Secondary: Jetbro LinkedIn, Jetbro Substack, newsletter.
- **Gating:** A selection of case studies (the most detailed, highest-impact ones) should be gated behind a form on the website — name, company, email, role. Ungated case studies are for awareness; gated ones generate leads.
- **Naming convention for gated case studies:** "[Industry] + [Outcome] + [Timeframe]" — e.g., "How a Mumbai Food-Tech Company Reduced Delivery Costs by 20% in 8 Weeks"

### 5.2 White Papers
Long-form, technical, authoritative. These are Jetbro's credibility anchors.

- **Format:** 2,000–5,000 words. Fully technical. Written for CTOs, Heads of Engineering, and senior architects.
- **Volume:** 1 per quarter
- **Topics (starting list):**
  - Event-driven architecture vs. record-driven: why it matters for AI-ready enterprises
  - The integration problem in Indian mid-market enterprises: patterns, failures, and what works
  - Building a company-wide AI ontology: the prerequisite nobody talks about
  - Why traditional ERPs fail digital transformation — and what the architecture should look like instead
- **Gating:** All white papers are gated. A download requires name, company, email, and role. Every download is a lead.
- **Where they live:** jetbro.in/resources (dedicated resources page to be built). Teased on LinkedIn and Substack with a link to download.
- **Production:** Rohan or Sharva provides the technical core. AI agent drafts. Internal marketer edits and formats. Rohan/Sharva final review.

### 5.3 Long-Form Articles
Opinion pieces and deep dives. Shorter than white papers, more accessible to a business audience.

- **Format:** 800–1,500 words. Business language. One strong argument.
- **Volume:** 1 per month
- **Where they live:** Published on Jetbro Substack first. Cross-posted to Medium and LinkedIn long-form. Linked from newsletter.
- **Topics:** Drawn from the content pillars defined in the Jetbro Thought Leadership Strategy.

### 5.4 LinkedIn Posts (Jetbro Company Page)
Short-form content for consistent brand presence and distribution.

- **Format:** Mix of text posts, carousels, and reposts with commentary.
- **Volume:** 4–5 per week
- **Content sources:** Original opinions, case study snippets, white paper teasers, industry commentary, behind-the-scenes.
- **Production:** AI agent drafts a batch of 5 posts weekly. Internal marketer reviews, refines, and schedules.

### 5.5 Filler Posts
Lightweight posts that keep channels active and the brand human. These do not need to map to a content pillar.

- **Format:** Short text post, image, or share — anything brief and genuine
- **Source:** Team learnings, things a team member read or discovered, observations, tools they tried, books, events — does not need to be enterprise tech or directly related to Jetbro's pillars
- **Volume:** As available — used to fill gaps in the posting schedule and maintain cadence between pillar posts
- **Tone:** Informal, first-person, conversational. Not promotional. No CTA required.
- **Platforms:** LinkedIn, Instagram, Twitter/X — wherever it fits naturally
- **Approval:** Rohan's approval still required, but turnaround expectation is same-day given the informal nature
- **Classification:** Tagged as "Filler" in the Content Calendar — not tracked against pillar KPIs

The purpose of filler posts is cadence and humanisation, not thought leadership. They signal that real people run this brand.

### 5.6 Weekly Newsletter
The core of Jetbro's owned audience strategy. Weekly, every week, without exception.

See **Section 6** for full newsletter structure and cadence.

## 6. The Weekly Newsletter

**Platform:** Jetbro Substack
**Audience:** Prospects, existing clients, industry peers, referral network
**Frequency:** Weekly (target day: Wednesday, 8am IST — mid-week, mid-morning)
**From name:** "Jetbro" or "Rohan at Jetbro" (test both in first month, keep whichever gets higher open rates)

### Newsletter Structure (every issue)

**Subject line formula:** One sharp, specific, curiosity-driving line. Not a summary of contents — a reason to open. E.g., "Why your ERP is slowing down your AI roadmap" or "The 20% cost reduction nobody asked for."

---

**Section 1: The Opening POV (150–200 words)**
One original observation, insight, or position from Rohan or the Jetbro team. Written in first person (Rohan's voice). Not a summary of the issue — a standalone thought worth reading even if the reader reads nothing else. This section earns the open for next week.

**Section 2: Featured Content (100–150 words)**
One piece of Jetbro content highlighted this week — a new case study, a white paper, a long-form article. One paragraph of context explaining why it is worth reading + a link. If the content is gated, say so clearly: "Download the full case study (free, takes 30 seconds)."

**Section 3: Industry Signal (3 items, 50 words each)**
Three curated links from the week — industry news, competitor moves, research, trends — each with a 1–2 sentence Jetbro commentary. Not neutral summaries. Jetbro's take on why each item matters (or doesn't) for Indian enterprises.

**Section 4: The Takeaway (50–75 words)**
One actionable idea, question, or prompt the reader can act on this week. Can be a framework, a question to ask their team, or a decision to make. Ends with an invitation to reply: "Hit reply and tell us what you think."

---

### Newsletter Production Workflow

```
Thursday (week N-1): AI agent drafts full newsletter based on content published that week
Friday:              Internal marketer reviews, edits, adds curated links + commentary
Monday:              Rohan reviews and approves (15 minutes — not a full edit, a quality check)
Tuesday:             Final version scheduled for Wednesday 8am send
Wednesday:           Newsletter goes out
```

**Backlog rule:** If content production slips in a given week, the newsletter still goes out. Keep a backlog of 4 pre-written "evergreen" issues (a strong case study, a timeless industry POV, a how-we-work piece, an FAQ issue) that can be published any week without being outdated.

### Building the Subscriber List

The list starts at zero. Growth comes from:
1. **Website signup** — prominent newsletter signup on jetbro.in homepage, resources page, and blog
2. **LinkedIn CTA** — weekly post on Jetbro company page and Rohan's profile pointing to the newsletter
3. **Existing network** — personal invite from Rohan and Sharva to their LinkedIn connections, past clients, and referral partners (one-time, done properly, not spam)
4. **Substack discovery** — Substack's recommendation network compounds over time
5. **Cross-promotion** — Rohan's personal Substack and Sharva's (when live) recommending the Jetbro Substack
6. **White paper downloads** — every gated download includes an opt-in to the newsletter (pre-checked, but honest)

**Target:** 100 subscribers in 90 days. 500 by end of FY.

## 8. Lead Generation Mechanism

Marketing at Jetbro must connect to sales. Every piece of content has a downstream action.

### Lead Capture Points

| Mechanism | Platform | What It Captures |
|---|---|---|
| Gated white paper download | jetbro.in/resources | Name, company, email, role |
| Gated case study download | jetbro.in/expertise | Name, company, email, role |
| Newsletter signup | jetbro.in + Substack | Email (+ optional company) |
| LinkedIn lead gen forms | LinkedIn | Name, email, company, role |
| Contact form | jetbro.in | Full inquiry — direct to sales |

### Lead Qualification & Handoff

Not every download is a sales lead. The internal marketer triages all new contacts weekly:

**Marketing Qualified Lead (MQL):** A contact who has downloaded gated content OR signed up for the newsletter AND whose company and role match any of the 4 Jetbro ICPs.

**Handoff trigger:** MQL is added to the Jetbro Lead Pipeline spreadsheet with source = "marketing" and stage = "Awareness." Rohan or the BD person reviews weekly and decides whether to initiate contact.

**Handoff process:**
1. Internal marketer identifies MQL
2. Adds to Lead Pipeline with full context (what they downloaded, when, company size, role)
3. Rohan reviews in weekly pipeline review
4. If ICP-fit: BD person sends a personalised, non-salesy outreach referencing the content they downloaded ("Glad you found the case study useful — happy to talk through how this applies to your context if helpful")
5. If not yet ICP-fit: stays in pipeline as a nurture contact, receives newsletter

## 9. The AI Agent — Role and Scope

The Jetbro AI agent (built on Claude Code) is not yet operational. When built, it handles the high-volume, repeatable parts of content production so the internal marketer can focus on quality, distribution, and strategy.

### What the AI agent does

| Task | Description |
|---|---|

| **LinkedIn post batch** | Drafts 5 LinkedIn posts per week for the Jetbro company page — a mix of formats (text, carousel script, repost commentary) |

### What the AI agent does NOT do

- Final approval of any content — Rohan or internal marketer always reviews before publishing
- Strategic decisions about what to publish or when
- Outreach or engagement with followers/subscribers
- Write white papers without a technical brief from Rohan or Sharva

### Build priority

The agent should be built to handle the **weekly newsletter draft** first — this is the highest-effort, most time-sensitive task. LinkedIn post batching is second. Everything else follows.

## 11. Budget

Jetbro's marketing function runs on zero paid spend. All channels — LinkedIn, Substack, Medium, website — are organic. No paid ads, no boosting, no external agency.

The only spend is optional tool subscriptions, adopted if and when the team decides they add enough value to justify the cost.

| Tool | Cost | Purpose | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canva Pro | ~₹4,000/month | Carousel design, white paper formatting, social graphics | Optional — adopt if design output justifies it |
| Buffer or Hootsuite | ~₹2,000/month | LinkedIn post scheduling | Optional — can schedule natively on LinkedIn for free |
| Substack | ₹0 | Newsletter + publication | Free |
| Medium | ₹0 | Cross-posting | Free |
| Claude Code / AI agent | Usage-based | Content drafting, repurposing, newsletter production | Core tool — cost absorbed into existing Claude Code usage |

**No paid ads.** Organic content is the entire distribution strategy. This is a deliberate choice — at Jetbro's stage, a well-written case study shared consistently outperforms a boosted post every time.

**Future consideration:** If marketing generates measurable pipeline and the team wants to accelerate, the first investment worth making is a fractional external marketer or a PR introduction — not paid ads.

A prospect or client mentions a specific piece of Jetbro content in a conversation. This is the signal that the content is landing with the right audience. Document every occurrence in the Sales Engagement Log.
